summary: Discussing challenges and opportunities in social robot navigation.
abstract: |
    Robots are gradually entering complex, lived-in spaces full of people and everyday activity, creating new challenges and opportunities for interaction and mobility. Yet the burden of adapting to the presence of robots still falls largely on people, who must accommodate them. One of the main barriers to progress in the field lies in how robot navigation is evaluated and optimised: current methods rely on simple statistics applied in the same way regardless of context, failing to account for the social dimensions of the navigation task and the environment. Navigating a crowded lift and a spacious airport, for example, call for very different behaviour and different evaluation criteria.
    
    In this seminar, Dr Manso will present his approach to benchmarking and optimising social robot navigation, with the broader goal of rethinking how navigation algorithms are built and assessed. At the heart of the approach are human opinions gathered through participant feedback, which ground state-of-the-art AI navigation techniques in real-world evidence. More broadly, the seminar will invite attendees to reflect on how a similar human-centred approach could be applied to their own HRI research.

Luis J. Manso is a Senior Lecturer (Associate Professor) in Computer Science at the College of Engineering and Physical Sciences of Aston University (joined in 08/2018) and a member of the Autonomous Robotics and Perception Laboratory.
Before that, he was a Research assistant (02/2010 to 07/2013) and Postdoctoral researcher (07/2013 to 07/2018) at the University of Extremadura. He obtained his degree in Computer Engineering and his PhD from the University of Extremadura in 2009 and 2013, respectively.

His research interests include geometric learning, active perception, human-robot interaction and sparse predictive world models.
